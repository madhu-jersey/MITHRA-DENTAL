Common problems

Common problems

Signs that it may be time to see a specialist.

Missing teeth

One or more gaps where teeth have been lost or removed.

Loose dentures

Dentures that slip, rub or make it hard to eat and speak.

Difficulty chewing

Trouble biting or chewing certain foods because of missing teeth.

Changes in facial shape

Cheeks or lips that look sunken after teeth have been lost.

Bone loss in the jaw

The jawbone shrinking where a tooth is missing.

Understanding the problem

Why they occur

Illustration: Tooth lossIllustration to be supplied
01

Tooth loss

Teeth can be lost to decay, gum disease or injury, leaving a gap that affects chewing and appearance.

Illustration: Jawbone shrinkageIllustration to be supplied
02

Jawbone shrinkage

A tooth root keeps the bone around it active. When the root is missing, the jawbone in that area slowly shrinks.

Risk factors

Causes

Common reasons these problems develop. Your doctor will look at what applies to you.

  1. Untreated tooth decay
  2. Periodontal (gum) disease
  3. Trauma to the mouth
  4. Long-term denture use

Many adults with missing teeth can have implants, but it depends on your bone, gum health and general health. The doctor will assess this with an examination and imaging.

Implants are placed under local anaesthetic. Some soreness and swelling afterwards is common, and the doctor will advise on pain relief and aftercare.

With good oral hygiene and regular check-ups, implants are designed to be a long-term solution. The crown on top may need replacing over time.
